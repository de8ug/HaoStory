# Why build a “global AI problem navigator” as one person?

UP2U started as an AI directory.

The contradiction soon became obvious. New models, tools, and projects appear around the world every day, and one person cannot cover them all. But turning the site into an open publishing platform would remove the part that makes it useful: this is a personal laboratory for testing how work changes in the AI era.

So the product position changed.

UP2U no longer tries to list every AI tool. It begins with real problems: What do people want AI to help them do? Which directions deserve an experiment? Which ideas have I tested myself, and which ones are still only worth watching?

The homepage now makes those boundaries explicit. The site is personally curated and verified. It offers problems, directions, project status, and links to finished work. Complete delivery lives where it fits best: video platforms, articles, GitHub repositories, or independent products.

UP2U does not pretend to deliver every answer. Its job is to connect worthwhile questions with credible experiments and outcomes.

This change also became HaoStory’s first production experiment.

While the discussion and development were happening, HaoStory captured the positioning change, the key decisions, a homepage screenshot, and the complete build/test result. The result is not a changelog. It is a story with a useful conclusion: limited personal capacity does not make a global perspective impossible. What must stay narrow is the promise.

One person can observe problems globally while taking responsibility only for what they choose, test, and publish.

That may be a more credible model for personal AI projects: do not imitate a large platform. Show why you chose something, how you tested it, where it failed, and what you will verify next.

UP2U now has a working loop across its homepage, positioning document, and project index, with the build and rendered-page tests passing. HaoStory produced a privacy-reviewed content package containing bilingual articles, short-video scripts, and an evidence index.

The changed judgment is simple: building in public should not be a promotional step after the project. It should be part of the project workflow.
